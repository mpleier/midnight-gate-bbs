                   SO YOU WANT TO RUN A BOARD

Everyone who has called a BBS for a year thinks about running one.
Most of them should not. Here is how to find out whether you are the
exception.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY COSTS
  A second phone line, every month, forever.
  A machine that can stay on in a hot room without complaint.
  A hard drive with room for other people's files.
  Your evenings. Not because the software needs you, but because the
  callers do.

THE SOFTWARE
  Every board package is a different opinion about how a board should
  feel. Pick one and learn it properly before you customise it. Most
  new sysops spend three weeks drawing menus and one afternoon on
  backups, and then lose the message bases to a bad sector.

  Whichever you choose: know how to restore from your own backup
  before you open the line. Not after.

THE RULES YOU WILL HAVE TO WRITE
  Write them down before you need them. At minimum:
    - one account per caller
    - no uploaded software you do not have the right to share
    - no posting of phone codes, card numbers, or other people's
      private business
    - what happens when someone breaks a rule

  Then apply them evenly, including to the loudest caller, including
  to your friends. The day you make an exception for a friend is the
  day the board starts to die.

THE THING NOBODY TELLS YOU
  A board is a small town with one policeman who also runs the
  general store. You will spend more time on other people's arguments
  than on your own hobbies. You will also get messages at 3am from
  someone who just needs to talk, and that part is worth it.

  If that trade sounds bad to you, be a caller. Callers keep a board
  alive. There is no shame in it.

THE FIRST WEEK
  Open with two nodes, not four. You will not have the traffic and
  you will want the spare line for testing.
  Post a welcome message before you open. Nothing is lonelier than a
  board with an empty message base.
  Set your time limits generously for the first month. You can always
  tighten them once people are hooked.

                        -- the sysop of the Gate
