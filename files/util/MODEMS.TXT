                   A CALLER'S GUIDE TO MODEMS
                        for the home board
                            rev. 1993

So you have a computer, a phone line, and a board you want to call.
Here is what the numbers on the box actually mean.

WHAT THE SPEEDS ARE
  300      The stone age. One page of text takes about a minute.
           Fine for messages. Forget files.
  1200     The hobby standard of the early eighties. Text flies,
           files crawl.
  2400     Comfortable for reading. Most boards still answer at this.
  9600     The first speed where ANSI art stops feeling like a slideshow.
  14400    The sweet spot for 1993. Error correction and compression
           make a real difference on noisy lines.
  28800+   Expensive, fast, and worth it if you call long distance.

WHAT THE LETTERS MEAN
  ARQ      Error correction. The modems re-send bad blocks, so a noisy
           line does not corrupt your download.
  LAPM     The protocol ARQ uses. You do not need to care, but boards
           like to print it.
  V.42BIS  Compression. Text compresses well. A .ZIP file does not, so
           do not expect miracles on files.
  V.32BIS  The standard for 14400.
  V.34     The standard for 28800 and 33600.
  V.90     The 56K standard. Only works when one end is digital, which
           is why your 56K modem connects at 33.6 to some boards.

PRACTICAL ADVICE
  - Buy an external modem with a row of status lights. When a call
    drops, the lights tell you whether it was the modem or the phone
    line. Internal cards tell you nothing.
  - A cheap surge suppressor is worth more than a fancy modem.
  - Do not put the modem on the same outlet as a refrigerator or an
    air conditioner. You will hear it in the retrains.
  - Keep a short phone cord. Long cords pick up noise.
  - If you get dropped every night at the same time, something in the
    house is turning on. Find it.

THE HANDSHAKE, BRIEFLY
  The board answers, both modems whistle at each other, they agree on
  a speed they both trust, and then you hear the hiss of data. If the
  whistling repeats, the line is bad and they are trying again at a
  lower speed. That is normal on a wet night.

  When you see NO CARRIER, the call is over. It is not an insult.

                       -- the sysop of the Gate
